Embedded Memory Lifecycle & Supply Continuity

Plan memory supply for 7–10-year embedded products using PCN, EOL, last-time-buy controls and qualified alternatives.

By OpenHMI Network · Published 25 October 2026

On this page Record the approved baselineFixed BOM and qualificationAct on PCN, EOL and LTB noticesMaintain a workable continuity plan

For a product expected to run for 7–10 years, plan for memory changes before production starts. Processor longevity does not guarantee the availability of every DRAM or flash part on the board.

Record the approved baseline

Keep the full memory ordering code, supplier, datasheet revision, temperature grade, package, die or firmware information where available, board revision and software configuration. Link these to qualification results and the approved sourcing channel.

Fixed BOM and qualification

A fixed BOM reduces uncontrolled substitutions. It does not prevent a supplier from changing a die, process, package material or flash controller firmware under a change-notification process. Ask what “fixed BOM” covers in writing.

Define which changes require document review, targeted regression or full qualification. A second source is useful only when its exact configuration has passed the relevant tests and remains available.

Act on PCN, EOL and LTB notices

Supply events and engineering actions
EventRequired action
PCN — product change notificationReview affected PNs and lots, technical impact, samples, validation scope and implementation date.
EOL — end of lifeMap exposure across products, service stock and forecast demand; assign replacement ownership.
LTB — last-time buyConfirm order and delivery deadlines, usable inventory, storage conditions and forecast risk before placing the final order.

Maintain a workable continuity plan

  • Name an owner for supplier notices and review them against active BOMs.
  • Review forecast, lead time and availability at planned intervals.
  • Reserve boards, samples, test fixtures and engineering time for requalification.
  • Keep a qualified alternative where practical; check its own lifecycle exposure.
  • Include repair and service demand, storage life and traceability in inventory decisions.

A longevity statement is an input to planning, not a substitute for program-specific supply terms. Start DRAM changes with second-source qualification; use the eMMC replacement checklist for managed flash.

Common question

Does a fixed BOM guarantee ten years of unchanged memory supply?

No. Define the supplier commitment, notification terms and controlled attributes. Maintain a qualification and replacement plan for changes or discontinuation.
